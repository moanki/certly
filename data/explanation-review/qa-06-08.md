## Critical

### 1. One keyed option combines release and emergency-stop controls

- **File:** `data/explanations/07-comprehensive-125-186.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e586001347473604`
- **Exact issue:** Option A, keyed correct, is `Emergency stop button / manual deflation button`. The explanation correctly says that the manual-release control initiates agent discharge, but also says an emergency-stop or abort control ordinarily inhibits release. It nevertheless opens by confirming the combined option. A learner could therefore treat an abort control as a discharge control in a life-safety system. The explanation cannot make the complete option safe or correct while the two opposite controls remain joined.
- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Keep A as the correct label, but change option A to **`Manual deflation/manual-release button`**. Use: “A is correct. The manual deflation, or manual-release, button is the on-site human control used to initiate the gas-extinguishing sequence. The fire cylinder stores the agent, the pressure switch reports discharge state, and the solenoid valve performs the commanded release. An emergency-stop or abort control must not be used to initiate discharge; it has a different safety function. Follow the approved cause-and-effect sequence, evacuate the protected zone, and verify the exact panel labels and site schematic before operation.”

## High

### 1. The explanation directly contradicts the keyed True answer

- **File:** `data/explanations/06-comprehensive-063-124.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e585001119fd9cf9`
- **Exact issue:** A is keyed True for “cycle life ... is direct proportional to depth of discharge,” but the explanation correctly states that deeper discharge generally reduces cycle count and that the relationship is inverse, not direct. “Keep the marked answer A” is answer-key preservation, not a rationale for A. This is internally contradictory instructional content.
- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Keep A as True but change the stem to **“The cycle life of a battery is inversely related to depth of discharge under otherwise comparable conditions.”** Use: “True. Deeper cycling removes more of the battery's capacity per cycle and generally causes greater electrochemical and mechanical stress, reducing the number of cycles available before end of life. Shallower cycles normally permit a higher cycle count. The exact relationship is not a simple universal formula because temperature, discharge rate, charging regime, battery chemistry, and the manufacturer's end-of-life criterion also affect cycle life; use the applicable battery cycle-life curves for design.”

- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Keep C and rewrite the stem to **“Before final commissioning, while the NetCol5000-A indoor and outdoor units are awaiting refrigerant charging or the assembled line is being pressure-tested, the refrigerant line contains ().”** Use: “C is correct: dry nitrogen is used to keep an uncommissioned refrigerant line dry and to pressure-test the assembled circuit for leaks. After the test, release the nitrogen safely, evacuate the circuit, verify vacuum integrity, and charge the specified refrigerant according to the model procedure. A completed operating circuit contains refrigerant, not nitrogen.”

## Medium

- **File:** `data/explanations/06-comprehensive-063-124.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e585001a3474b75e`
- **Exact issue:** The described continuous constant-voltage maintenance mode is float charging, but A says only “Charging.” The explanation is technically sound, yet calling A merely the “closest available choice” leaves the item imprecise and makes the keyed choice look provisional.
- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Keep A correct and change option A to **`Float charging`**. Equalized charging temporarily uses a higher voltage to restore charge and reduce imbalance; it is distinct from the lower continuous float-charge setting. Apply the value only to the supported battery configuration and follow the battery manufacturer's voltage, temperature, and duration limits.”
