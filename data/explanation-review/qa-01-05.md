## Critical

### 1. Unsafe reversal of the gas-release warning

- **File:** `data/explanations/05-comprehensive-001-062.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e58100204fd64f91`
- **Exact issue:** The stem says the indicator displays **“Done enter while releasing gas”** and A is keyed True. The explanation silently reinterprets that text as “personnel must not enter.” A learner sees a literal instruction to enter during extinguishing-agent release, followed by confirmation that the statement is true. The later safety discussion does not cure the contradiction. This is malformed, safety-critical content and must not be migrated as written.
- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Correct the quoted indicator text in the source question to **“Do not enter while gas is being released.”** Then use: “A is correct. The gas-release indicator warns personnel not to enter while extinguishing gas is being discharged. Personnel must evacuate and may re-enter only after the responsible fire-safety authority confirms that discharge is complete and the space is safe.” If the source question cannot be corrected, remove this item from the migration set; an explanation-only change cannot make the current quoted sentence safely true.

## High

### 1. The keyed altitude option contains an unsupported failure mechanism

- **File:** `data/explanations/02-ups2000-ups5000.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e5760014da0a0aab`
- **Exact issue:** C is keyed correct, but it combines the valid reduced-air-density/heat-dissipation rationale with “capacitor inside UPS inflation.” The explanation explicitly says the capacitor-inflation clause is not a standard explanation and should not be treated as the mechanism. It therefore does not fully justify the whole keyed option. Huawei's current UPS5000-E specification requires derating above 1000 m under IEC 62040-3 but does not support the capacitor-inflation claim: [Huawei UPS5000-E technical specifications](https://info.support.huawei.com/Energy/info/en_US/all/docView/EDOC1100267070?lang=2).
- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Change option C to: **“At high altitude, lower air density reduces cooling effectiveness and dielectric strength, so the UPS must be derated.”** Proposed explanation: “C is correct. Air density and dielectric strength decrease with altitude. Reduced air mass flow weakens heat removal, while reduced dielectric strength narrows insulation margins. Derating limits losses and electrical stress so the UPS remains within its validated operating envelope. Use the model-specific altitude curve; capacitor inflation is not the governing explanation.”

### 2. The explanation answers a different statement about battery configuration

- **File:** `data/explanations/02-ups2000-ups5000.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e5760026d7160c4f`
- **Exact issue:** The stem says the **number of battery strings** is adjustable, but A is keyed True and the explanation admits that the intended feature is the adjustable **number of batteries within each string**. These are different parameters: Huawei documents both “Batteries in a battery string” and “Number of battery strings” separately, and the UPS5000-A electrical specification gives supported battery counts per string: [Huawei battery parameters](https://info.support.huawei.com/DpinfoAppDoc/pro_erp_slice001/doc/neteco/ups/en/en-us_topic_0000001738826517.html), [Huawei UPS5000-A manual](https://info.support.huawei.com/Energy/info/en_US/all/docView/EDOC1100036335?lang=2). The explanation cannot truthfully call the current statement True by substituting a different feature.
- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Change the stem to: **“The number of batteries in each UPS5000-A battery string can be adjusted, helping avoid replacement of an otherwise serviceable whole string when a supported battery-count reconfiguration is appropriate.”** Proposed explanation: “A is correct. UPS5000-A supports an approved range of series battery counts. The configured count must match the physical string so charging voltage, end-of-discharge thresholds, and capacity calculations remain correct. This flexibility does not permit mixing battery models, ages, capacities, or conditions.”

### 5. EPO is presented as electric-shock protection even though it is not isolation

- **File:** `data/explanations/05-comprehensive-001-062.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e581003343f102e3`
- **Exact issue:** D says EPO is triggered “to protect loads against electric shock,” which is malformed because loads do not receive electric shock. The explanation rewrites this as protecting personnel and suggests EPO reduces the chance that circuitry remains energized, then later admits EPO does not replace isolation. Huawei defines EPO as a command that stops UPS power supply; it does not establish absence of voltage or isolate UPS inputs: [Huawei UPS5000-E EPO behavior](https://info.support.huawei.com/Energy/info/en_US/all/docView/EDOC1100267070?lang=2). The opening claim can teach unsafe commissioning practice.
- **Precise correction (answer label unchanged):** Change D to: **“To verify the EPO circuit and command all applicable UPS outputs to stop supplying power during the prescribed commissioning test.”** Proposed explanation: “D is correct. Triggering the EPO input verifies that the configured emergency command is received and that the applicable UPS outputs stop supplying power. EPO is an operational shutdown command, not electrical isolation. Lockout/tagout, isolation of every source, discharge waiting time, and absence-of-voltage verification remain mandatory before electrical work.”

## Medium

### 1. The maintenance-bypass explanation is unnecessarily speculative

- **File:** `data/explanations/01-smartli-ups-basic.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e572000af62c8810`
- **Exact issue:** B and D are keyed correct, but the explanation says D merely “can be interpreted” as correct and leaves the record marked for review. Huawei's procedure is more precise: first transfer manually to bypass, then close maintenance bypass; Huawei also states that EPO removes UPS output but the maintenance-bypass path continues supplying the load when that path is closed: [Huawei UPS5000-E maintenance bypass procedure](https://info.support.huawei.com/Energy/info/en_US/all/docView/EDOC1100267070?lang=2). The current hedge makes a safety sequence sound uncertain.
- **Proposed corrected explanation:** “B and D are correct. For a no-break transfer, first command the UPS to bypass mode and verify that the bypass source is normal; then close the maintenance-bypass switch. If the procedure also requires EPO, trigger EPO only after maintenance bypass is closed, because EPO stops UPS conversion output while the closed maintenance-bypass path continues feeding the load. EPO is not a routine substitute for the controlled bypass transfer and does not prove internal isolation.”

### 2. “Compatible with module and tower” is explained with invented generalities

- **File:** `data/explanations/02-ups2000-ups5000.json`
- **Appwrite id:** `6aa2e576001df8484ee6`
- **Exact issue:** The explanation never defines the keyed statement in product terms. It says UPS5000-A can “accommodate deployment requirements associated with both modular-system and traditional tower-UPS applications,” then lists generic design checks. Huawei instead describes UPS5000-A as a transformerless **tower-mounted** UPS using a unit-level power design with an embedded maintenance-bypass switch: [Huawei UPS5000-A product description](https://info.support.huawei.com/Energy/info/en_US/all/docView/EDOC1100036335?lang=2). The current text is vague and may imply physical module/chassis interchangeability.
- **Proposed corrected explanation:** “A is correct in the intended product-positioning sense. UPS5000-A is a transformerless tower-mounted UPS, but its power section uses a unit-level design and it includes an embedded maintenance-bypass switch for service with the load supplied through bypass. It therefore combines a tower architecture with selected maintainability characteristics associated with modular systems. This does not mean UPS5000-E power modules fit an UPS5000-A cabinet or that the two product architectures are physically interchangeable.”

This is a style improvement, not a migration blocker by itself.
